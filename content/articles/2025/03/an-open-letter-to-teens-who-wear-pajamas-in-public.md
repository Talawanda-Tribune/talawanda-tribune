Dear pajama-loving teens,

I am what remains of a society with standards. And apparently there are very few of us left. Everywhere I go, I see teenagers, sometimes even grown adults, walking around in pajamas, clearly unaware that all of society mocks them behind their backs.

Do you wake up and think “I want to look like an absolute slob today”? Do you ask yourself, “How can I look as lazy as possible”? You look like you rolled out of bed in a zombie-like trance.

When I get ready in the morning I try to not look like an uncouth, vulgar heathen. Apparently you guys take the opposite approach. Honestly, it might as well be an Olympic event with the amount of talent it takes to look like that. If you really want to wear pajamas you might as well wear the pink bunny suit from Christmas Story.

It is a simple task to wear normal, socially acceptable clothes. Now, maybe you were in a rush in the morning, while quite a lame excuse, there is a solution. You could pick out your clothes before you get into your PJs and fall asleep. Maybe you can go to your local wal-mart and find some nice, comfortable clothes to wear.

There is a reason pajama day exists, a time when societal conventions are circumvented, to provide a fun, enjoyable day. It is not an open opportunity for you to flaunt these conventions everyday, to the chagrin of your peers.

I have nothing against pajamas, but I do have something against you. I’ll concede that pajamas are comfortable, but it is juvenile to wear them in public.

A civilized society has no room for cretins such as yourselves, the fact that Darwinism hasn’t yet purged you from this Earth is a miracle in its own right. Creatures, even at the microscopic level, change themselves based on their environment. How simple must you be to not match a single celled organism in the functions of adaptability? We, as a society, state that we make progress as we become more comfortable, as we change standards, and as we lower them. This is not progress, this is a door we cannot close.

When the time comes we must ask ourselves why we are alive? To be functional? To be comfortable? To be useful? No. We have to be better, better than the people who came before. We developed ways to clothe ourselves early in our civilization. We must not regress from the generations which raised us. We have to be better, we have to wear pajamas in the bedroom.
