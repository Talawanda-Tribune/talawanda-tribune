There are many different ghost stories and urban legends that take place in the small college town of Oxford, OH. Many would debate that this town is a very nice quiet peaceful town based off of the nice scenery but the town’s urban legends beg to differ.

One of the most famous is the ghost biker. One night a man on a motorcycle was in a bit of a hurry, he was on his way to propose to his girlfriend who was living on Earhart Road. As most people about to propose you feel mixed feelings of terror and excitement and doubt whether or not your significant other will say yes or no, you might even think that this might not be the right thing right now, so considering that you can imagine the type of emotions this man was feeling. On his way to her house he missed a sharp turn and he flew off the road and was decapitated by a barbed-wire fence. Today it is said that death did not deter the man, and that he is still trying to reach his girlfriend. The legend is that if you want to see him you have to drive to the girlfriend's old Earhart road home, currently owned by Mr. Falk, and park facing south, then flash your headlights three times, then you may see the headlights of the man’s motorcycle focusing straight ahead, only to disappear when he approaches the fatal curve. If the headlight does not disappear you are recommended to drive away quickly in the opposite direction.

The next story starts at the place called Fisher hall, which was formally an asylum back in the 1880s. The owner of the asylum decided to build a little tunnel so he could get through to places without his patients seeing him, later Miami acquired this property in 1925. During coming years, students would stumble upon straitjackets. One day, on a regular day a student named Ronald Henry Tammen, he was an active student in the dance band and belonged in Delta Tau Delta fraternity, and also a member of the wrestling team. At 8:00 Sunday evening, April 19, 1953 Tammen left his hall to get bed sheets due to someone putting a fish in his bed, then returned to his dorm to study. He was last seen alive at 10:30 pm. His roommate later found his psychology book laying out which wasn't odd, he just figured he went to the fraternity to study or something, worry started to occur when he didn't return the next day. Today Tammen’s death is still unknown, some people think he was murdered others feel he might still be alive. One witness claims that she talked to him but he was in a weird state, he did not remember his name and asked her for directions to the nearest bus stop. In 1973 police Butler county coroner revealed that tammen visited his office, seeking a blood test, five months to the date of his disappearance. As time passes it becomes less and less likely that we will ever know what happened to him.

The last story is about a beloved professor named Henry Snyder. Snyder was a very popular faculty member. He had a wife named minni who was the complete opposite of him. She would usually perform a classical and gypsy repertoire without her husband, sometimes with her own band. In August 1898 Henry fell ill with what was called “heat prostration” and when he returned the following month he was clearly not himself. On September 14, professor Snyder ingested a fatal dose of potassium cyanide in his Bryce Hall laboratory. Many people at the time thought Snyder had committed suicide, later Snyder’s wife had said that he was dealing with mental health problems due to over working. Sometime after his death Minni found a new husband named William Pugh, Pugh was a recent Snyder’s lab assistant and held a background in chemistry and a knowledge in poissons. After their marriage William walked out on Minni in June 1919 but yet never to be seen since then. Was both of her husbands deaths a coincidence or did she have something to do with it?
